# V2 baseline: how right are check and lint today

Measured on 22 Oct 2026 with `@dualkit/eval`. This is the starting line for v2. Every number below is a count over private data, so no site, product or snippet is named.

## What was measured

Precision: 20 products (10 repos for lint, 10 recorded pages for check), every finding hand labelled real, handled, intended or unclear. Unclear findings are left out of the figure.

Recall: 65 cases. Eight bug kinds, eight clean pages each, plus one real miss. Each case is a clean page with one Arabic bug planted in it. A tool catches a case if it reports something new on the broken element. No cases were dropped.

## Against the pass bars

The spec asks for at least 90% of findings real, for both tools, and at least 90% of the miss set found.

| measure | bar | check today | lint today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| findings that are real, warnings and errors | 90% | 76% | 59% |
| findings that are real, all severities | 90% | 63% | 34% |
| miss set found | 90% | 31% | 0% |

Neither tool is close. Check is nearer on precision. Lint is wrong four times in ten on warnings and about two thirds of the time once notes count. Lint catches none of the planted bugs.

Lint reads source files. The planted bugs sit in single-file pages, and none of them trip a source rule. That is a limit of what lint looks at, not a bug in one rule. A source check cannot see a rendered date order, a back arrow, or the width of a number.

Check caught every tracking and number isolation case, and half of the date order cases. It caught none of the other five kinds. Check has rules for tracking (type/arabic-tracking) and number isolation (bidi/unisolated-number). It also has rules for mirrored icons and punctuation flips, and they caught nothing in this set. otp-digits is behavioural, so a page looks the same until someone types Arabic digits. Those cases are kept anyway, because the tools still should catch them.

## Check against the 21 Oct figures

On 21 Oct lint was at 34% and check at 80%. Today lint reads 34% when every severity counts, the same basis as before. Check reads 76% on warnings and errors. The gap closes to exactly 80% if the two layout rules left out on 21 Oct are arabic-ltr-block and clipped-text, which would match the note that those two were set aside. I have not confirmed that those were the two. Both rules report as warnings in this run. The rules package was last changed on 21 Oct, the same day as the earlier figures, so I cannot show that the tools are identical.

## What was dropped

Nothing. All 20 products had a sample file and ran. All 65 cases rendered differently from the clean page, or belong to the two kinds where a render cannot show the bug (otp-digits and arabic-tracking).

## What this means for v2

- Check precision needs about 14 points. Fixing arabic-leading and clipped-text moves it the most.
- Lint precision needs a rule-by-rule cleanup, and a decision on whether source rules should judge intent they cannot see.
- Recall is the larger gap. Neither tool caught a single case in five of the eight kinds.
